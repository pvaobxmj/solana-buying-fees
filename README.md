Buying Solana takes about five minutes. The part that decides whether you paid $100 or $104.50 for the same SOL is not the buying — it's the payment rail you picked and the order type you used. Most beginner guides walk you through the screens and skip that part entirely.

Here's what you actually need before starting: a government ID, one working payment method, and a decision about where the SOL will live after you buy it. ## Three places to buy SOL, and what each one really costs

You can buy Solana through a centralized exchange, through a fiat on-ramp built into a wallet, or on-chain via a DEX or bridge. The difference in cost between the cheapest and most expensive route is roughly 5% of your purchase — the same SOL, different invoice.

| Route | Typical cost | Speed | Setup needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Centralized exchange (spot market) | 0.10% taker at the base tier, plus whatever the deposit rail charges | Minutes after funding | Account + KYC |
| In-wallet fiat on-ramp (MoonPay, Transak, Stripe) | Roughly 3–5% all-in once spread is included | Minutes to hours, depending on KYC | Card details only |
| DEX or cross-chain bridge | ~0.1–0.5% bridge fee plus gas on the origin chain | Minutes to about an hour | A funded wallet on another chain |

The on-ramp route is the fastest way to own your first SOL and the most expensive per dollar. It makes sense if you have no crypto anywhere yet and you're buying a few hundred dollars' worth. If you're moving four figures, the percentage stops being a rounding error and an exchange order book starts looking a lot more attractive.

### Step 4: Place the SOL order

Go to the spot market, search SOL, and pick a pair. SOL/USDT is the deepest; SOL/USDC also works if that's what you're holding.

You have two order types:

- **Market order** — fills immediately at whatever the book offers. Fast, and it pays the taker fee.
- **Limit order** — you name your price and wait. If it rests on the book instead of filling instantly, it becomes a maker order, and on Gate that's the cheaper side of the spread once your account reaches VIP4 and above.

For a first purchase, a market order is fine. For anything larger, a limit order a few basis points inside the spread often fills within minutes and costs less.

## Moving your SOL off the exchange

Leaving SOL on an exchange is fine for trading. It's not the same as owning it. If you want self-custody, the withdrawal process has three failure points.

**The network.** SOL withdrawals must go to a native Solana address. If an exchange offers SOL on multiple networks and you pick an ERC-20 or BEP-20 wrapped version, the funds land somewhere your Solana wallet can't see. Check the network selector every single time.

**The memo field.** Solana doesn't use destination memos for ordinary transfers. If a form shows one, leave it alone unless the receiving service told you otherwise.

**The address.** Solana addresses are base58 strings roughly 32–44 characters long. Compare the first four and last four characters against your wallet before confirming, and send a small test amount the first time you move a large sum.

Typical exchange withdrawal fees for SOL run around 0.01–0.02 SOL, roughly $1–2, plus nothing extra on Solana's side since the network itself is cheap. Gate's withdrawal fees are per-coin and adjust with network conditions, so the number you see at withdrawal time is the real one. Withdrawal limits scale with your VIP tier — 3,000,000 USD per 24 hours at VIP0, rising to 50,000,000 at VIP16.

A standard Solana wallet setup: install the wallet, write the seed phrase on paper and store it offline, never type it into a website, never photograph it. Anyone with that phrase has your SOL, and no support desk can reverse it.

## Five mistakes that cost people money

1. **Buying $200 with a card and eating 5%.** That's $10 for the convenience of not waiting a day for a bank transfer. Fine if you value speed at that price, less fine if you didn't notice.
2. **Market-ordering into a thin pair.** SOL/USDT is deep. SOL/ETH or SOL/some-other-quote may not be. Check the order book depth before hitting market.
3. **Withdrawing to the wrong network.** The single most common way to lose funds permanently, and it's a dropdown you have to read.
4. **Skipping 2FA and the anti-phishing code.** Both are free and both are set up in under a minute. The phishing code in particular is what lets you spot a fake email instantly.
5. **Treating bonus credits as cash.** Reward vouchers and trading credits usually come with conditions. Read what you got before you count it as profit.

Yes, on Gate and most major exchanges, usually through a third-party processor. Card purchases carry roughly a 1–5% fee depending on the provider and region, and they settle in minutes. Bank transfers are cheaper but take 1–3 business days.

There's no large minimum on the spot market, but practical floors come from the funding rails — card processors and C2C sellers each set their own minimums, and withdrawals have minimums too. Buying $20 with a 5% card fee is a real cost; buying $500 is the same percentage of a bigger number.

### How long does the whole process take?

Account creation and KYC are typically done the same session. The variable is funding. Card funding is minutes, bank transfer is days, on-chain deposit is minutes once you've sent the crypto.

### Do I have to complete KYC?

For any meaningful purchase volume, yes. Verification unlocks higher limits and more payment channels, and regulated exchanges are required to collect identity information. Requirements vary by region and method.
